How to Level Up Your Performance

When you carry out a performance review, you decide what you will do differently next time. But that plan is biased by hindsight, locking you into a never-ending cycle. What separates the best from the rest isn't a better plan for next time. It's the ability to get it right this time.



What the audience will be able to do by the end of the session

•	Think “this time” instead of “next time”

•	Feel confident before and during a performance

•	Do the skill the moment requires

What’s it about?

According to Gallup, only 14% of employees strongly agree their performance reviews inspire them to improve.

In business, as in life, nothing ever goes entirely to plan. 



Plan-Do-Review does what it says on the tin. Except every review is built on a fatal flaw that we can judge a plan by its outcome. You can perform poorly and still win. You can perform brilliantly and still lose. So why do we keep grading ourselves on results we can't actually control, and treating unpredictable moments as if they were obvious all along?

It's a three-step framework built on the psychology that underpins every high-stakes moment: the daily habits that form your foundation, the preparation that sharpens your edge, and the in-the-moment tools that carry you through when it counts. Along the way, Darren names the three warning signs of underperformance every person and team needs to catch before it's too late.

Audiences leave with a new lens on performance: less obsessed with outcomes, more in command of process — and a framework they can trust, whatever the scoreboard says. Because the scoreboard was never the whole story.
