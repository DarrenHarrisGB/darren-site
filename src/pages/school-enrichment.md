Try this before you read on! Close your eyes and ask a colleague to guide you to the staffroom door using only words. Notice how quickly you start listening, and how much depends on how clearly they speak. Now imagine doing that with a ball that rattles when it rolls, and 30 voices instead of one. That's the blind football experience your pupils get.

## 2,000 Participant, 0 Injuries
Keep children safe with a session built on control, not contact.

I'll be honest: the kids really want to play a match. But keeping children safe is my priority. Anyway, they soon realise to control, pass and dribble the ball blindfolded is challenging enough. There's no tackling, and players call out "voy", Spanish for "I'm going", whenever they move towards the ball, so everyone knows where everyone else is.

## Where it fits in your Curriculum
- PE and sport: pupils experience a new sport, broadening the range of activities your PE and sport premium is there to support.
Wear blindfolds to build physical literacy, relying on sound, touch, and spatial awareness to orientate and navigate the pitch.
- Spoken language: guiding a partner demands precise instructions and careful listening, every second they play.
- Personal development and PSHE: pupils build empathy, trust, teamwork and resilience, and see disability through a new lens.
- Inclusion: every child takes part, whether playing blindfolded or guiding a partner.
